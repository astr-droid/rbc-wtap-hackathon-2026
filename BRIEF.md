# Compass: team brief

Every career starts with a conversation. Compass makes sure it continues.

RBC hackathon, Fri 2026-11-20. Team of 3. 7-minute pitch plus Q&A.

**Terms.** Early talent means the co-ops and new grads. The program team is the staff who run the early talent program. The key person is the person RBC already assigns to bring a new hire into things (to confirm with the Early Talent lead).

## The prompt

> Create a digital solution that captures the voice of early talent participants and surfaces meaningful insights about their experience, whether related to mentorship, onboarding, team dynamics, or career development. Your tool should help identify students at risk of leaving, highlight systemic improvements needed in our programs, and enable proactive support.

## The answer in one paragraph

Compass starts a conversation with every co-op and new grad in their first week, introduced by their key person or program contact. A short chat captures their goals, worries and how they like feedback, and turns that into an **Ambition Card** they own. Their manager gets a one-page brief with realistic ways to fit those goals into the work already on the team. Short check-ins every two weeks track whether the goals are happening. **Chronicle**, the insights engine, turns anonymous patterns across the cohort into stories RBC can act on: what is missing, where, and what to change.

All of it lives on **one map**. The same career journey helps the student, the manager and the program team, and each of them sees it at their own zoom level. The map is also a **living career story**: what the student wants, what they have tried, who they have met, and the case for why they should stay. It carries over when a co-op returns as a new grad.

## The interface: one map

The product is a single, centralized map drawn like a nautical chart. It replaces three separate dashboards. Everyone opens the same map and sees their own layer of it.

- **Center:** the person. Their Ambition Card is the pin in the middle.
- **Destinations:** their goals (product management, AI and machine learning, leadership) drawn as places on the chart, with a route from where they are now.
- **Terrain:** RBC teams and roles from the org chart, drawn as regions.
- **People:** key person, program contact, mentors and manager as pins, with lines showing who is connected to whom. This answers the lead's pain point about visibility and who is connected, literally.
- **Progress:** each check-in moves a marker along the route. A route that has stalled is a goal that is not happening yet.
- **Stops:** each route has stops. A **coffee chat** with a volunteer mentor (15 minutes, one tap to book) or a **workshop** (welcome-to-tech, team workshops, upskilling). Each stop is open, booked, done, or waiting on someone.

Three zoom levels of the same map:

| Zoom | Who | What the map shows |
|---|---|---|
| Me | Early talent member | Their own routes with coffee chat and workshop stops, their own participation (for example, 2 of 3 stops done, 1 waiting on you), nearby people worth meeting, and the follow-up button |
| My team | Manager | Their co-op's approved goals as routes, with suggested project stops along each one. Nothing private |
| The cohort | Program team | Everyone's goals pooled and anonymous. Regions glow where many want to go and few teams offer a way in (9 want AI, 3 teams offer it, 2 mentors, 1 workshop). Chronicle stories are pinned to the map where they apply. Goals stay anonymous and groups under 5 are hidden. A named outreach queue shows who has gone quiet |

Private check-ins, reflections and anonymous concerns never appear on the team or cohort layers.

**Why a map:** a career is a route between where you are and where you want to be. One picture works for all three audiences. It makes "who is connected" visible instead of anecdotal. And the demo becomes one continuous zoom out, from one student to the whole program.

The map is an abstract chart, not geography. Nodes, routes and regions are drawn from the synthetic tables.

## How Compass covers every line of the prompt

| Prompt says | Compass does |
|---|---|
| Captures the voice of early talent | Conversational intake and 10-second check-ins that adapt to the person, plus a "share a win / ask for help" button available any time |
| Surfaces meaningful insights | Chronicle turns grouped data into short stories with counts and an evidence drawer, pinned on the cohort map |
| Mentorship, onboarding, team dynamics, career development | Onboarding: Ambition Card and week 1 manager brief. Career development: routes on the map and project suggestions. Mentorship: coffee chat stops with volunteer mentors, and workshops. Team dynamics: manager brief and midterm prep |
| Identify students at risk of leaving | Two signals, neither a score. A stalled route, which the student sees first. A quiet stretch (no participation for 4 weeks), which prompts a supportive message from the program team. Students who ask for follow-up go to a named program contact |
| Highlight systemic improvements | Chronicle stories end with a suggested program change and track whether it worked |
| Enable proactive support | Compass offers a connection, a talking point or a mentor chat before the student has to ask. Follow-up requests go to a named program contact |

**Decision to confirm as a team:** voice is private, participation is visible. What a student writes (check-ins, reflections, concerns) stays private and shows only as anonymous patterns. What a student does (coffee chats booked and done, workshops attended, check-ins answered) is shown by name to the student and the program team, the same way event attendance already is. No individual gets a hidden score. If a judge asks about a named at-risk list, RBC can choose that as policy.

## Coffee chats, workshops and the living career story

**Stops on a route.** A coffee chat is a 15-minute conversation with a volunteer mentor who opted in ahead of time and shared when they are free, so students are not chasing people who may ghost them. A workshop is an existing session, such as welcome-to-tech, a team workshop or upskilling. Each stop shows its state on the map: open, booked, done, or waiting on someone.

**Responsiveness shows on both sides.** If a mentor replied and the student has not answered, the student sees "waiting on you" on their own map. If a mentor never replies, that shows too, in the program team's view. Ghosting is visible from both directions.

**Voice is private, participation is visible.**

| | Student | Manager | Program team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Check-ins, reflections, concerns | Own, in full | Nothing | Anonymous patterns only |
| Participation (chats, workshops, check-ins answered, mentor replies) | Own, in full | Only the stops the student shares | By name, for outreach |
| Goals | Own | Approved goals only | Pooled and anonymous |

Participation is the kind of fact RBC already records for events. It is shown by name so the program team can send a supportive message to someone who has gone quiet. It is not a score, and it never includes anything the student wrote privately. The student sees exactly what the program team sees.

**Return offer.** From co-op to new grad, the map continues. The student can attach their career story (goals, stops completed, wins) to the return conversation and choose what goes in. Whether participation counts toward a return offer is an RBC policy decision. We show how it would work and leave the policy to them.

**Demand and supply on the cohort map.** Chronicle compares what students want with the mentors and workshops available. Example: 9 want AI, 2 mentors and 1 workshop cover it. The one-click actions are to recruit mentors or run a workshop.

## What each person sees

**Early talent member** (zoom: Me)
- A 3-minute chat on joining, with multiple choice for people who prefer it. Output: the Ambition Card (career interests, skills to build, experiences wanted, concerns, feedback style). They can edit it anytime and choose what the manager sees.
- Check-ins every 2 weeks tied to their own goals. Example: "You wanted to explore product management. Have you had a chance?" If no, Compass suggests someone to talk to, a way to raise it with their manager, or an opportunity.
- Their routes on the map: 3 realistic paths from their goal, and the people at RBC on those paths.
- Before the midterm: talking points and questions built from their own wins and goals.
- A button to request a follow-up from a named program contact. They choose whether to reveal who they are.

**Manager** (zoom: My team)
- A one-page brief at onboarding and before the midterm: the student's approved goals, plus small project suggestions that fit the current role (for example, a developer interested in product management joins feature-planning discussions). Accept, adjust or ignore.
- Managers never see private check-ins, reflections or anonymous concerns.

**Program team** (zoom: The cohort)
- Anonymous cohort map. Groups under 5 are hidden.
- Chronicle stories, a follow-up queue, and one-click actions (recruit mentors for a gap, draft a midterm prep workshop, post an FAQ).

## Chronicle example

Illustrative, using simulated data.

1. **Pattern:** 9 of 25 students want AI or machine learning. Only 3 teams offer related work. On the cohort map, the AI region glows with few routes in.
2. **Story:** "High interest, limited opportunity." Several students say their day-to-day work leaves no room to explore it. Evidence drawer shows the 9 and the 3, plus a counter-story from students who found a way in.
3. **Action:** introduce AI mentor matching and ask managers to find small AI tasks inside existing roles.
4. **Follow-through:** later check-ins show whether more routes into AI are moving.

Other stories to seed: "The week 3 confidence dip", "The Vancouver connection gap", "The midterm disconnect" (students and managers expect different things from feedback).

## Judging criteria

**Solution development: onboarding, career development, program-level developments. Relevant. Check notes.**
- Onboarding: Ambition Card in week 1, manager brief right after.
- Career development: routes on the map, coffee chats, workshops, project suggestions, connections.
- Program level: Chronicle, the cohort map, follow-up queue.
- Notes from the Early Talent lead, all covered: feedback all the time, why people leave, follow-up flag, point-in-time feelings turned into data, real-time support, stories from data, more honest feedback, many layers of early talent (co-op and new grad versions), visibility of who is connected, performance conversation as the first moment a problem shows up.
- Audience is AI group co-ops and new grads up to 2 years after graduation, not WTAP.

**Innovation and integration: fits into existing tools.**
- Runs in the chat tool early talent already use (Teams, Slack or Webex, confirm with the lead). The map opens from a link in that chat.
- Lines up with tools RBC already uses: coffee chats book through the Outlook calendar and meet on Webex, workshops are the sessions the program team already runs, and points of contact come from Workday. Uses existing program data (event attendance, post-event feedback, mentor participation).
- Exports a one-page summary a manager can attach to the existing review form. It supports the review and does not replace it.
- No new login, no new platform to roll out.
- A technical path into RBC: a Workday report read as a web service, Outlook calendar invites through Microsoft Graph, a Webex link, read-only access, and no write-back to HR systems. See the technical section.
- What is new: the conversation starts from the student's ambition in week 1, the manager is briefed before problems show up, and one map serves the student, the manager and the program.

**Business viability: reasonably implementable, not cost prohibitive.**
- Pilot with one cohort. No new headcount.
- Each AI step is a short text generation per person per check-in. Measure the actual cost per student from our n8n execution logs before the demo and quote that number.
- Replaces time the program team already spends on end-of-term surveys, collecting anecdotes and manual follow-up.
- Privacy by design: aggregates only, groups under 5 hidden, no reading of email, calendar or chat, student controls sharing.
- Be honest in Q&A: we have not validated cost or time saved with RBC. It is a pilot hypothesis.

## Technical implementation into RBC

**Principle:** Compass adds one new surface, the map. It reads from and writes to tools RBC already runs. Everything below is a proposed design to confirm with RBC IT. At the hackathon, each connection is a synthetic table or a mock.

**How the pieces fit:** the student, manager and program team open the map through RBC single sign-on and get nudges in the chat tool. Behind the map, the Compass service runs the workflows and calls an RBC-approved model. It connects to Workday, the Microsoft calendar, Webex and the program team's event records. It keeps two separate stores: a voice store (private, anonymous patterns) and a participation store (named, for outreach).

| Connection | How it would work | Access | Hackathon stand-in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Workday** | A custom report (org, manager, title, start date) is enabled as a web service. The Compass service reads it with an integration system user. The start date triggers the week 1 intro | Read-only | Synthetic org chart table |
| **Microsoft calendar** | When mentor and student agree, Compass creates an Outlook calendar invite through the Microsoft Graph calendar API | Creates invites only, with IT approval | Mock invite |
| **Webex** | The invite carries a Webex link. Microsoft's built-in online meeting option covers Teams and Skype, so the link comes from Webex, for example through the Webex Scheduler add-in for Outlook. Whether RBC has it deployed is to confirm | Link only | Mock invite showing a Webex link |
| **Workshop and event records** | Sign-ups and attendance the program team already keeps are imported. Workshops appear as stops, and attendance marks them done | Read-only | Synthetic workshop table |
| **Chat tool** | Check-in nudges and the link that opens the map. Same backend whichever chat tool the AI group uses | Send messages | Faked chat window |
| **Sign-in and model** | RBC single sign-on for the map. The workflows call whichever model RBC approves | Standard access | Our own model access, synthetic data only |

**Phases**
- **Hackathon, today:** Clickable map on synthetic data. Every connection is a synthetic table or a mock. Pre-generated content with one live model call.
- **Pilot, one cohort for one term:** Workday read-only, calendar invites, Webex links, workshop records imported. No write-back to HR systems. Hosted inside RBC and through RBC's privacy and security review.
- **Scale:** More early talent programs on the same map, once the pilot shows what to keep and what policy RBC wants on participation.

**Questions we expect from RBC IT, and our answers:**
- Where is the data hosted, and does it stay inside RBC?
- What is the least access each connection needs?
- Is there an audit log of who saw what?
- Which model is approved for internal use, and what is retained?
- Does it need a privacy impact review before a pilot?
- Our answers: read-only wherever possible, calendar invites are the only write, two separate stores, hosted inside RBC, and nothing real moves until the privacy and security review is done.

**Confirmed by the team:** Microsoft calendar, Webex meetings, Workday for points of contact. **Not confirmed:** the chat tool the AI group uses, and whether the Webex Scheduler add-in is deployed.

## Q&A prep

- **How is this different from existing review tools?** Those start at review time and run on manager input. Compass starts in week 1 and runs on the student's goals, so the manager walks into the midterm already informed.
- **Isn't showing participation surveillance?** It shows what people did, not what they said, and it is the same kind of fact RBC already records for events. The student sees exactly what the program team sees. Voice stays private and anonymous, so honest feedback is not affected.
- **Won't students feel pressure to perform?** It is possible, which is why there is no score, no private content in the view, and the student controls what goes into a return conversation. Whether participation counts toward a return offer is RBC's call.
- **How does this connect to return offers?** The map continues from co-op to new grad. The student can attach their career story to the return conversation and choose what is in it.
- **How would this actually connect to Workday and Outlook?** Workday can expose a custom report as a web service, which Compass reads with an integration user. For coffee chats, Compass creates an Outlook calendar invite through Microsoft Graph, and the Webex link comes from Webex.
- **What does RBC IT have to do?** Enable one Workday report and an integration user, approve calendar access, confirm the Webex link method and the chat tool, and run the usual privacy and security review. Nothing is written back to HR systems.
- **Why a map?** A career is a route from where you are to where you want to be. One picture serves the student, the manager and the program, and it makes who is connected visible.
- **How do you find at-risk students without surveillance?** We look for a persistent gap between a stated goal and experience, shown first to the student. Program staff see anonymous counts and only the people who asked for follow-up.
- **Will students answer honestly?** Anonymous by default, student controls what is shared, and answers get a useful response straight away, which reduces survey fatigue.
- **Will managers use it?** The brief is one page, arrives at moments they already care about, and suggests small changes to existing work.
- **What does it cost?** Short AI text generation per person per check-in, plus the chat tool RBC already has. Quote the measured number from our logs. Not yet validated with RBC.
- **Does it work for new grads?** Same flow, longer-term goals and development paths instead of four-month objectives.
- **What about small cohorts?** Groups under 5 are hidden. Stories show counts, time period and a counter-story, and do not claim cause.
- **Is the data real?** No. Everything is simulated and labelled as such.
